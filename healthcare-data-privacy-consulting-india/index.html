Why healthcare privacy needs its own approach

Healthcare organisations handle diagnoses, test results, prescriptions, insurance details and images, often across clinical systems, labs, apps and third parties. Staff access, shared devices and outsourced vendors add risk. A breach harms patients directly and damages trust that is hard to rebuild.

Laws and frameworks that commonly apply

  • India's DPDP Act: applies to digital personal data, including health data, processed in India. It requires notice, valid consent or another permitted ground, security safeguards, breach reporting and respect for individual rights. See our DPDP compliance page.
  • Children's data: the DPDP Act requires verifiable parental or guardian consent for children's data, which affects paediatric, school-health and family-oriented services.
  • Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM): organisations that join it follow its health data management policy and technical requirements.
  • GDPR: health, genetic and biometric data are special category data. Clinical trials, medical tourism and services for EU patients can bring you into scope. See GDPR consulting.
  • HIPAA and US requirements: if you serve US covered entities as a business associate, you will face contractual security and breach requirements.
  • Research ethics and sector rules: clinical research and medical records carry additional confidentiality duties that sit alongside privacy law.

Where healthcare programmes usually have gaps

  • Consent and notices that do not match how data is actually used in registration, billing and apps.
  • Broad staff access to patient records without logging or review.
  • Vendors such as labs, telemedicine tools and billing partners without proper agreements.
  • Retention of records with no clear schedule, and unclear handling of deletion requests against medical-record duties.
  • Cross-border sharing for trials or second opinions without a transfer assessment.

How we help

  • Gap assessment: against the DPDP Act and any GDPR or HIPAA obligations, with a prioritised roadmap.
  • Data mapping: patient journeys, systems and vendors, from registration to billing and archiving.

Frequently asked questions

Is health data treated differently under the DPDP Act?

The DPDP Act does not create a separate category for sensitive data, but the sensitivity of health data affects the safeguards you need and the risk of harm. Other laws and contracts may add requirements.

Do we need patient consent for treatment records?

The DPDP Act allows processing on consent or on certain specified legitimate uses. Which applies depends on the activity, so we assess each purpose rather than rely on one blanket form.

Does the GDPR apply to a hospital in India?

It can, for example if you offer services to people in the EU or monitor their behaviour, or process data for EU trial sponsors. We confirm this in a scoping review.

Can we keep records if a patient asks for deletion?

Deletion requests have to be balanced against legal retention duties for medical records. We help you define retention rules and a response approach.

Can you help us respond to a hospital partner's privacy questionnaire?

Yes. We prepare standard answers and the evidence behind them.
