Privacy Risk Assessment: A Practical Guide

A simple, repeatable way to score privacy risk and decide when to run a full DPIA.

Published 8 November 2026 · By PrivaGlobe privacy consulting team, Mumbai · General information, not legal advice

Quick answer

A privacy risk assessment looks at how a project or system could harm people through its use of personal data, scores the risk, and sets controls. A full DPIA is for higher-risk processing.

Five steps

1

Describe the processing: data, purpose, systems, people.

2

Identify what could go wrong for individuals.

3

Score likelihood and impact.

4

Choose controls and record residual risk.

5

Get sign-off and set a review date.

When to run a full DPIA

  • Large volumes of data or sensitive data
  • Children's data
  • New technology or large-scale monitoring
  • Processing that is likely to be notified as significant under DPDP

Significant Data Fiduciaries must conduct periodic DPIAs under Rule 13. Others can use the same method for high-risk projects. See privacy governance and DPIA.

Questions

Is a risk assessment the same as a DPIA?

A DPIA is a structured, documented form of risk assessment for higher-risk processing.

Who should do it?

The project owner, with privacy, security and legal input.

How often should we repeat it?

When the processing changes, and on a schedule for high-risk systems.

Need a method and templates? Privacy governance and DPIA.
