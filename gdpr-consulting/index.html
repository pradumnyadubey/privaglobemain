Quick answer

Yes, if you offer goods or services to people in the EU or EEA, or monitor their behaviour, even without an office there. Fines can reach €20 million or 4% of worldwide turnover, and breaches must be reported to the regulator within 72 hours where feasible.

Does the GDPR apply to a company outside Europe?

The General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 has extraterritorial reach. Under Article 3 it applies to organisations outside the EU or EEA when they offer goods or services to people in the EU, or monitor their behaviour, for example through analytics, tracking or profiling. An Indian SaaS company with European customers, an outsourcing provider handling EU client data, or an e-commerce store that ships to Europe can all be in scope.

The UK has its own version, the UK GDPR, alongside the Data Protection Act 2018. The rules are close to the EU's but not identical, particularly on international transfers, so we scope the UK separately where it is relevant.

Core GDPR obligations

  • Lawful basis (Article 6): every processing activity needs one of six bases: consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public task or legitimate interests. Choosing and recording the right one is the foundation of the programme.
  • Special category data (Article 9): health, biometric, genetic, political, religious and similar data need an additional condition and stronger safeguards.
  • Transparency (Articles 12 to 14): clear privacy notices explaining what you collect, why, who receives it and how long you keep it.
  • Individual rights (Articles 15 to 22): access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection, normally answered within one month.
  • Records of processing (Article 30): a documented record of your processing activities, which regulators routinely ask to see first.
  • Data protection impact assessments (Article 35): required for high-risk processing such as large-scale profiling or sensitive data.
  • Processors and contracts (Article 28): a data processing agreement with every vendor that handles personal data for you.
  • Security and breach notification (Articles 32 to 34): appropriate security and, for notifiable breaches, notice to the supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware.
  • International transfers (Chapter V): moving personal data out of the EEA needs a transfer mechanism such as an adequacy decision or Standard Contractual Clauses. India does not currently benefit from an EU adequacy decision, so data flowing between EU entities or customers and India normally relies on contractual safeguards and a transfer assessment.
  • EU representative (Article 27): organisations outside the EU that fall within scope often must appoint a representative inside the Union.
  • Data Protection Officer (Articles 37 to 39): mandatory in specific cases. See our DPO as a Service page.

GDPR fines

Supervisory authorities can impose administrative fines of up to €20 million or 4% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher, for the most serious infringements such as breaching core processing principles, individual rights or transfer rules. Lesser infringements, such as failing to keep records or notify a breach, carry fines of up to €10 million or 2% of turnover. Beyond fines, regulators can order you to stop processing, and individuals can claim compensation. Enterprise customers also increasingly ask for evidence of GDPR compliance before signing contracts.

Frequently asked questions

Does the GDPR apply to my Indian company?

It applies if you offer goods or services to people in the EU or EEA, or monitor their behaviour, even without an office there. It also applies if you act as a processor for a company that is subject to it. We can confirm this in a short applicability review.

Do we need to appoint an EU representative?

Often yes, if you are outside the EU and your processing falls under Article 3(2). There are limited exemptions for occasional, low-risk processing. We assess whether you need one and help you arrange it.

Is transferring data from the EU to India allowed?

Yes, with a valid transfer mechanism. India does not currently have an EU adequacy decision, so organisations typically use Standard Contractual Clauses together with a transfer impact assessment and supplementary measures where needed.

How quickly must we report a breach under GDPR?

Where a breach is likely to result in a risk to individuals, you must notify the supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware. High-risk breaches also require notifying affected individuals.

Is GDPR compliance a one-off project?

No. Records, assessments, vendor contracts and training need regular review as your products, vendors and the law change. Many clients keep us on a light retainer or use our DPO service after the initial project.

Do you also cover the UK GDPR, CCPA and other laws?

Yes. We cover the UK GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand PDPA and Saudi PDPL, applying one control framework across them.
