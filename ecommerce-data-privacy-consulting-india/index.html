Data Privacy Consulting for E-commerce and Retail

Online retail runs on customer data: accounts, addresses, payments, browsing and marketing lists. DPDP changes how you ask for it, keep it and delete it.

Last reviewed: 8 November 2026 by the PrivaGlobe privacy team

Schedule a consultationRequest a DPDP assessment
Quick answer

E-commerce businesses collect a lot of customer data and share it with logistics, payment, marketing and analytics partners. DPDP requires clear notices, valid consent for non-essential uses, retention limits, safeguards and a way for customers to exercise their rights.

Where e-commerce programmes focus

  • Consent for marketing and tracking: separate, withdrawable choices and a record of them.
  • Retention (Rule 8): large e-commerce entities have a defined erasure duty after a period of user inactivity, with advance notice. We confirm whether it applies to you.
  • Partners: logistics, payments, ad-tech and support vendors under proper contracts.
  • Children: where your audience may include minors, verifiable parental consent under Rule 10.
  • Rights and grievances: a clear route to access, correct or erase, and a published contact.
  • Security: safeguards for accounts and payment-adjacent data.

See consent management and data discovery for the building blocks.

This page is general information, not legal advice. Laws and official dates change, so we confirm the current position for your situation at the start of every engagement.

Frequently asked questions

Does the retention rule apply to every online shop?

The specific erasure rule is aimed at larger e-commerce, gaming and social media entities, defined by user-number thresholds. We check where you stand.

Do we need consent for order emails?

Order and delivery messages usually rest on the purchase itself. Marketing messages need their own valid consent.

What about cookies?

Non-essential cookies and tracking should rest on clear consent. We help design the banner and the record.
