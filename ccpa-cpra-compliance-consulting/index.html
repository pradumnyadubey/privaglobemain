Quick answer

The CCPA applies to for-profit businesses that do business in California and meet any one threshold: annual gross revenue above US$25 million (adjusted for inflation), buying, selling or sharing the personal information of 100,000 or more California consumers or households, or earning 50% or more of revenue from selling or sharing personal information.

Does the CCPA apply to a company outside California or the US?

The California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA), as amended by the California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA), applies to for-profit businesses that do business in California and collect personal information about California residents, and that meet at least one threshold: annual gross revenue above the statutory limit (US$25 million, adjusted for inflation), buying, selling or sharing the personal information of 100,000 or more consumers or households, or earning 50% or more of annual revenue from selling or sharing personal information. Physical presence in California is not required.

An Indian SaaS company with Californian customers, an outsourcing provider that processes data for a US business, or an online store shipping to California can fall within scope or be bound through contracts. The law covers customers, website visitors, employees, job applicants and business contacts.

Core CCPA and CPRA obligations

  • Notice at collection and privacy policy: tell people what categories of personal information you collect, why, whether you sell or share it, and how long you keep it.
  • Consumer rights: to know and access, delete, correct, opt out of sale or sharing, limit the use of sensitive personal information, and not be discriminated against for exercising rights. Requests are generally answered within 45 days, with a possible extension.
  • "Do Not Sell or Share" and "Limit the Use of My Sensitive Personal Information" links: clear opt-out mechanisms, including honouring opt-out preference signals such as Global Privacy Control.
  • Service providers and contractors: written contracts with the terms the law requires, so that disclosures to vendors are not treated as a sale or sharing.
  • Purpose limitation and data minimisation: collection and use must be reasonably necessary and proportionate to the disclosed purposes.
  • Reasonable security: the law gives individuals a private right of action for certain data breaches caused by a failure to maintain reasonable security.
  • Risk assessments, cybersecurity audits and automated decision-making: newer regulations from the California Privacy Protection Agency add duties in these areas for some businesses. We track the current timelines and tell you what applies to you.

Penalties and enforcement

The California Privacy Protection Agency and the California Attorney General enforce the law. Administrative fines and civil penalties can reach US$2,500 per violation, or US$7,500 per intentional violation or violation involving minors, with amounts adjusted over time. Each affected consumer or each incident can count as a separate violation, so exposure grows quickly. US enterprise customers also routinely ask vendors for evidence of CCPA readiness.

Frequently asked questions

Does the CCPA apply to my Indian company?

It can, if you do business with California residents and meet a threshold, or if you process data as a service provider for a business that is subject to it. A short applicability review gives a clear answer.

What is the difference between the CCPA and the CPRA?

The CPRA amended and expanded the CCPA. It added rights to correct data and limit the use of sensitive personal information, created the California Privacy Protection Agency and tightened rules on sharing data for advertising. In practice, people refer to the combined law as the CCPA or CCPA/CPRA.

Do we need a Do Not Sell or Share link?

If you sell or share personal information, yes. Many websites share data through advertising and analytics tools without realising it, so we review your trackers before deciding.

Does this cover employee data?

Yes. Since 2023 the law applies to employee, applicant and business-contact data as well as consumer data.

Can you help if we are only a vendor to US companies?

Yes. We prepare service provider terms, security answers and evidence so you can pass customer reviews.
