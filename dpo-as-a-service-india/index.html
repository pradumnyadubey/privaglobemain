What does a Data Protection Officer do?

A Data Protection Officer (DPO) is the person responsible for overseeing an organisation's data protection compliance. The role advises leadership, monitors compliance, supports impact assessments, acts as the contact point for regulators and individuals, and promotes a privacy-aware culture. The role needs legal, technical and operational understanding, plus enough independence to give honest advice, which is hard to find in a single internal hire.

When do you need a DPO?

Under India's DPDP Act

A Data Protection Officer based in India is required for Significant Data Fiduciaries, the organisations the government designates based on factors such as the volume and sensitivity of personal data processed and risk to individuals. The DPO must represent the fiduciary under the Act, be responsible to its board or equivalent governing body, and act as the point of contact for grievance redressal. Other Data Fiduciaries are not required to appoint a DPO, but they must publish the business contact details of a person who can answer questions about data processing. Many choose a DPO anyway to show accountability to customers and regulators.

Under the GDPR

Article 37 requires a DPO where you are a public authority, where your core activities involve regular and systematic monitoring of individuals on a large scale, or where your core activities involve large-scale processing of special category or criminal-offence data. The GDPR allows a DPO to be an external service provider, and requires that the DPO is independent, reports to the highest level of management and has no conflict of interest. That last point is why the Chief Technology Officer, head of marketing or head of HR usually cannot hold the role.

When it is simply good governance

Enterprise customers, investors and auditors increasingly ask who owns privacy in your organisation. A named DPO with documented responsibilities and a monthly reporting rhythm is a clear answer.

Frequently asked questions

Can a DPO be outsourced?

Under the GDPR, yes: Article 37 expressly allows a DPO to be an external service provider. The DPDP Act requires a Significant Data Fiduciary's DPO to be based in India and answerable to its board, so we confirm the arrangement for your situation before appointment.

Is a DPO mandatory in India?

Only for Significant Data Fiduciaries under the DPDP Act. Other organisations must publish a contact person for data protection queries, and many appoint a DPO voluntarily.

What is the difference between a DPO and a compliance consultant?

A consultant delivers a defined project, such as a gap assessment or documentation. A DPO provides ongoing, independent oversight and acts as the formal contact point. Many clients start with a project from PrivaGlobe and then continue with the DPO service.

Who can be a DPO?

There is no fixed qualification list, but the DPO needs sound knowledge of data protection law and practice, and must be free of conflicts of interest. Senior executives who decide how data is used generally cannot hold the role.

Does appointing a DPO make us compliant?

No. A DPO oversees and advises, but compliance depends on your organisation putting controls into practice. The DPO helps you do that and keeps evidence of it.
