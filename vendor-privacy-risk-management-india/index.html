Vendor Privacy Risk Management for DPDP and Global Laws

Your vendors handle your customers’ data, but the accountability stays with you. We build a vendor programme that is proportionate, documented and easy to run.

Last reviewed: 8 November 2026 by the PrivaGlobe privacy team

Schedule a consultationRequest a DPDP assessment
Quick answer

Under the DPDP Act a Data Fiduciary remains responsible for personal data processed on its behalf by a Data Processor. Vendor privacy risk management means knowing who your processors are, tiering them by risk, putting the right contract terms in place and reviewing them over time.

The vendor programme

1

Inventory every vendor that touches personal data.

2

Tier them by data sensitivity, volume and access.

3

Send a proportionate questionnaire and review the answers.

4

Update contracts with processing terms, security duties and breach notice.

5

Review high-risk vendors on a schedule and at renewal.

What goes into a processor contract?

  • Purpose and instructions for processing.
  • Security safeguards matching Rule 6.
  • Prompt notice of any breach so you can meet Rule 7 timelines.
  • Sub-processor approval, deletion on exit and audit rights.
  • Cross-border transfer terms where data leaves India.

The PrivaGlobe platform can hold the vendor register, questionnaires and review dates. Related reading: vendor risk management and data protection.

This page is general information, not legal advice. Laws and official dates change, so we confirm the current position for your situation at the start of every engagement.

Frequently asked questions

Do we have to audit every vendor?

No. Tier vendors by risk and spend most effort on the few that hold sensitive data or have wide access.

What if a vendor refuses to sign new terms?

We help you assess the risk, negotiate reasonable wording, or plan a move to a vendor who will.

Does this also cover GDPR?

Yes. The same register supports GDPR processor terms, with extra clauses where those rules apply.
