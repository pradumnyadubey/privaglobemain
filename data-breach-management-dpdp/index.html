Data Breach Management and Response Planning under DPDP

When a breach happens you have little time. A tested playbook, clear owners and ready-made templates let you respond calmly and meet regulatory duties.

Last reviewed: 8 November 2026 by the PrivaGlobe privacy team

Schedule a consultationRequest a DPDP assessment
Quick answer

Under Rule 7 of the DPDP Rules a Data Fiduciary must tell each affected Data Principal without delay and report to the Data Protection Board, with a detailed report following within 72 hours. A breach playbook turns that into named owners, steps and templates you can use in the first hour.

What a good breach playbook contains

  • Detection and triage: who logs an incident, and how severity is judged.
  • Decision rights: who can declare a breach, and who speaks to the Board, regulators and customers.
  • Notification templates: for the Board and for affected people, in plain language.
  • Evidence and logs: what to preserve so you can explain what happened.
  • Vendor links: how processors alert you, and by when.
  • Lessons learned: a review that feeds fixes into your risk register.

How we help

We draft the playbook with your IT and security teams, run a tabletop exercise with leadership, and test that your vendors can meet their notice duties. DPO as a Service clients can also have us on call during an incident. See breach management and regulatory responsibilities.

Penalty context. Failing to take reasonable security safeguards to prevent a breach can attract a penalty of up to ₹250 crore under the DPDP Act schedule. Failing to notify of a breach falls under a lower ceiling.

This page is general information, not legal advice. Laws and official dates change, so we confirm the current position for your situation at the start of every engagement.

Frequently asked questions

Do all incidents have to be reported?

The Rules require reporting of personal data breaches. We help you judge which incidents qualify and record the reasoning either way.

Who should lead breach response?

A named incident lead with authority across IT, legal and communications, supported by the privacy lead or DPO.

How often should we rehearse?

At least once a year, and after any major system or vendor change.
