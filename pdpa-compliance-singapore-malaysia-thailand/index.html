Quick answer

Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand each have their own PDPA with different regulators, breach rules and penalties. Singapore requires a designated DPO and breach notice within 3 calendar days of assessment, Thailand requires breach notice within 72 hours, and Malaysia's 2024 amendments added a mandatory DPO and breach notification.

Singapore PDPA

Singapore's PDPA governs how organisations collect, use and disclose personal data. Its core obligations are consent, purpose limitation, notification, access and correction, accuracy, protection, retention limitation, transfer limitation, data breach notification and accountability. It also includes the Do Not Call registry rules for marketing messages. Organisations must designate a DPO and publish contact details, and must report notifiable breaches to the PDPC within 3 calendar days of assessing them.

Malaysia PDPA

Malaysia's PDPA was amended in 2024 to bring it closer to international norms. The changes include a mandatory DPO, mandatory breach notification, a right to data portability, direct obligations for data processors, treatment of biometric data as sensitive and higher penalties. Because the amendments take effect in stages, we confirm which provisions apply to you at the start of every engagement.

Thailand PDPA

Thailand's PDPA has applied in full since 1 June 2022 and has extraterritorial reach, covering foreign organisations that offer goods or services to people in Thailand or monitor their behaviour there. It requires a lawful basis for processing, stricter rules for sensitive data, a DPO in specific cases, rights for individuals and notification of reportable breaches to the regulator within 72 hours.

Frequently asked questions

Do Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand have the same PDPA?

No. Each country has its own Personal Data Protection Act, regulator, breach rules and penalties. They share principles such as consent, purpose limitation and security, but a programme must be aligned to each law.

Is a Data Protection Officer required in Singapore?

Yes. Organisations must designate at least one individual to be responsible for PDPA compliance and make their business contact information available.

How fast must we report a data breach in these countries?

In Singapore, notify the PDPC as soon as practicable and no later than 3 calendar days after assessing that a breach is notifiable. Thailand requires notice within 72 hours of becoming aware of a reportable breach. Malaysia's amended PDPA also introduced mandatory breach notification.

Can one programme cover all three countries?

Yes. We build one control framework for notices, consent, rights, security and vendors, then add the local steps each law requires.
