Data Discovery and Mapping: Know Where Personal Data Lives

You cannot protect, delete or report on data you cannot find. We map where personal data enters, moves, rests and leaves, then keep that map current.

Last reviewed: 8 November 2026 by the PrivaGlobe privacy team

Schedule a consultationRequest a DPDP assessment
Quick answer

Data discovery and mapping identifies what personal data you hold, where it is stored, why you hold it, who can access it and where it is sent. It underpins notices, retention, rights requests, breach response and vendor oversight.

What the map shows

  • Systems, owners and the categories of personal data in each.
  • Purpose and lawful ground for each use.
  • Retention periods and how deletion happens.
  • Vendors and cross-border transfers.
  • Risk rating, so remediation goes where it matters.

How we build it

1

Interview process owners and review system lists.

2

Record data categories, purposes and recipients.

3

Optionally scan connected sources to find unknown data.

4

Hand over a living register your teams can update.

The register feeds your gap assessment, notices and retention rules. Read data mapping: the first step towards DPDP compliance.

This page is general information, not legal advice. Laws and official dates change, so we confirm the current position for your situation at the start of every engagement.

Frequently asked questions

Do we need a tool for data mapping?

Not to start. A well-run spreadsheet works for many organisations. Tools help when you have many systems or need continuous scanning.

How often should the map be updated?

Whenever you add a system, vendor or purpose, and reviewed at least once a year.

Does mapping find all personal data?

Interviews find the processes you know. Scanning helps find files and fields you did not know about.
