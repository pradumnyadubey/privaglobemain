DPDP vs GDPR: Key Differences for Businesses

India's DPDP Act and the EU's GDPR share a rights-based core but differ in the details that decide your compliance workload. This is a practical side-by-side comparison.

Last reviewed: 28 October 2026 by the PrivaGlobe privacy team

Quick answer

Both laws protect personal data and give individuals rights, and both apply to foreign companies serving their residents. The biggest differences: DPDP has no legitimate interests basis and no special category of sensitive data, treats everyone under 18 as a child, and caps penalties at ₹250 crore, while the GDPR allows fines up to €20 million or 4% of worldwide turnover.

Where DPDP is simpler than the GDPR

DPDP has fewer rights, no special category of sensitive data and no general legitimate interests basis to document. A single notice and a valid consent can cover most processing. There is also no requirement for records of processing in the GDPR sense, although you still need to know what you hold to meet your duties.

Where DPDP is stricter

  • Children: everyone under 18 is a child, a higher bar than the GDPR's default of 16, and tracking or targeted advertising to children is restricted.
  • Consent: because there is no legitimate interests basis, more processing depends on consent that must be as easy to withdraw as to give.
  • Government powers: the state can restrict transfers to certain countries and can designate Significant Data Fiduciaries with extra duties such as an India-based DPO and independent audits.

What this means if you serve both markets

Build one data inventory, one rights workflow and one vendor contract programme, then layer the differences on top. Keep GDPR-style records and impact assessments, add DPDP-specific notices and parental consent for under-18s, and set up a breach playbook that meets both timelines. Frequently asked questions

Is the DPDP Act the same as the GDPR?

No. Both give individuals rights over their data, but the DPDP Act is simpler and built around consent plus a short list of legitimate uses. The GDPR has six lawful bases, special categories of sensitive data, and more individual rights.

If we comply with the GDPR, are we compliant with DPDP?

Not automatically. A GDPR programme gives you a strong head start on notices, records, security and vendor contracts, but DPDP has its own rules on consent, children under 18, breach reporting and the Data Protection Board. A gap assessment shows what is missing.

Does DPDP have a legitimate interests basis like the GDPR?

No. The DPDP Act relies on consent or a defined list of legitimate uses, such as voluntarily provided data for a stated purpose, legal obligations and certain employment purposes. It has no general legitimate interests test.

Who is a child under DPDP and under the GDPR?

Under DPDP, anyone under 18 is a child. Under the GDPR the default age for consent to online services is 16, and each EU member state can lower it to as low as 13.
