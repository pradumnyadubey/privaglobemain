DPDP Readiness Checklist: What to Complete Before 13 May 2027

Work through these eight areas to get ready for India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act. Tick off each item and keep evidence as you go.

Last reviewed: 28 October 2026 by the PrivaGlobe privacy team

Quick answer

Start with a data inventory, then fix notices and consent, set up rights and breach processes, handle children's data and vendor contracts, and keep evidence. The consent manager framework starts on 13 November 2026 and the main obligations on 13 May 2027.

1. Know your data

  • List every system, spreadsheet and vendor that holds personal data of people in India.
  • Record what you collect, why, where it is stored, who can see it and how long you keep it.
  • Work out your role for each activity: Data Fiduciary or Data Processor.
  • Check whether you could be designated a Significant Data Fiduciary based on volume and sensitivity of data.

2. Notice and consent

  • Rewrite privacy notices so they itemise the data collected, the purpose, how to exercise rights and how to complain.
  • Offer the notice in English and, where relevant, in the languages your customers use.
  • Rebuild consent so it is specific, unambiguous, given by a clear action and as easy to withdraw as to give.
  • Identify processing that relies on a legitimate use instead of consent, and document why.
  • Plan for consent managers, whose framework starts on 13 November 2026.

3. Rights and grievances

  • Set up a way for people to request access, correction and erasure and to nominate someone to act for them.
  • Publish the contact details of a person who can answer questions about processing.
  • Define a grievance process with named owners and response times.

4. Security and breach response

  • Put reasonable security safeguards in place and keep evidence of them.
  • Write a breach playbook covering detection, assessment, notifying the Data Protection Board and affected people, and the detailed report to the Board.
  • Run a breach drill with your technical, legal and communications teams.

5. Children's data

  • Identify any processing of data of people under 18.
  • Introduce verifiable parental consent where it applies.
  • Stop tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising aimed at children, unless an exemption in the Rules covers you.

6. Retention and erasure

  • Set retention periods for each category of data and delete data once its purpose is served.
  • Keep what a law requires you to keep, and document the law.

7. Vendors and processors

  • List all processors and review their contracts for DPDP-aligned clauses on security, breach reporting and deletion.
  • Remember that you stay responsible for data your processors handle.

8. Governance and evidence

  • Assign an owner for privacy and report progress to senior management.
  • Train staff in roles that handle personal data.
  • If you may be a Significant Data Fiduciary, plan for an India-based Data Protection Officer, an independent data auditor and periodic impact assessments.
  • Keep dated evidence of notices, consents, assessments and training so you can show your work.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to become DPDP ready?

It depends on your size and data landscape. A small company with few systems may need weeks, while an enterprise with many vendors may need several months. The main obligations apply from 13 May 2027, so starting now leaves a workable window.

Is this checklist enough to be compliant?

It covers the core areas, but compliance depends on how each item is applied to your business. A gap assessment turns the list into a scored report with priorities.
